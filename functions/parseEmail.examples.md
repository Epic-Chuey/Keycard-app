# Keycard email parsing — real-world examples

Reference examples for improving the free-form field extraction in
`parseEmail.js` (`extractFields`, `extractLocation`, `extractLabeled`, etc).

These are raw "Original Request" bodies from actual property managers/facility
leads — NOT generated by the Email Request tool (see `email-request.html`),
so they don't match `KEYCARD_ENTRY_LINE_RE`/`KEYCARD_REQUEST_ANCHOR_RE` and
fall back to the general-purpose heuristics. Each example records the raw
body plus what a human reads out of it, so the heuristics can be tuned
against real cases rather than guessed at. Collect a handful before changing
`parseEmail.js` — one example isn't enough to generalize a new label pattern
from without risking a regression on the existing ones.

## Example 1 — Keith Arline III, 3950 E Airport Dr, Ontario, CA

Raw body (from the dashboard's "View Original Request" modal):

```
Hello,

Can we please activate 1 keycard for Rocket Express at 3950 E Airport Dr. Ontario, CA Facility?

Company Name: Rocket Express
Facility: 3950 E Airport Dr.- Ontario

Cardholder Name: Mario Magana
Keycard Number: 2105866490
Access: Warehouse

_______________
Keith Arline III | Facility Lead
3950 E Airport Dr | Ontario | California | 91761 | United States of America
Phone: (626) 803-6538 | keith.arline@cubework.com
www.cubework.com

NOTICE: This message, and any attachments, contain(s) information that may be
confidential or protected by privilege from disclosure...
```

Expected extraction (what a human would fill into the Email Request form):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Cubework Location | 3950 E Airport Dr. Ontario, CA |
| Tenant Company Name | Rocket Express |
| Tenant First/Last Name | Mario Magana |
| Keycard Number | 2105866490 |
| Access | Warehouse (custom "Specify" value, not Standard) |

Notes for the parser (implemented):
- `Company Name:` now checked ahead of the bare `"Name"` label in `company`'s
  label list, so it can't lose to a `Cardholder Name:` line that happens to
  appear earlier in some other email.
- `Facility:` added to `extractLocation()`'s label list, alongside
  `Location`/`LOCATION`/`Property`.
- `Cardholder Name:` now has its own `cardholderName` field, separate from
  `company`.
- Added `stripSignatureBlock()`, which cuts the text at the first `___`/`--`
  divider line before any labeled-line/regex lookup runs, specifically so a
  signature's own `Phone:`/address lines are never read as the tenant's.
- Added a generic `access` field (`extractLabeled(["Access"])`) for free-form
  emails, distinct from `requestAccess` (the Email Request tool's own
  generated-body re-parse).

## Example 2 — Noelani Napoleon, Rio Grande Service Center LLC

Raw body (from the dashboard's "View Original Request" modal):

```
Hi,
Can you please activate a new keycard for Rio Grande service center LLC.

David Driver -1586761460

Noelani Napoleon | Property Associate

2470 N. Airport Blvd Unit A | Aurora | Colorado

United States | Direct +1 (657) 465-4385

noelani.napoleon@cubework.com

www.Cubework.com | LinkedIn


image NOTICE: This message, and any attachments, contain(s) information that may be
confidential or protected by privilege from disclosure...
```

Expected extraction:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Tenant Company Name | Rio Grande Service Center LLC |
| Tenant First/Last Name | David Driver |
| Keycard Number | 1586761460 |
| Cubework Location | missing — not stated anywhere in the body |
| Cubework or Unis (serves) | missing — not stated |
| Access | missing — not stated |
| Tenant Email | missing — only the sender's own email appears, not the tenant's |
| Tenant Phone | missing — only the sender's own phone appears, not the tenant's |

Notes for the parser (implemented):
- No labels at all this time — company is named inline in the opening
  sentence ("...keycard for Rio Grande service center LLC.") and the
  cardholder is paired with the keycard number on one bare line
  ("David Driver -1586761460"). Added two label-less fallbacks, each only
  tried when the labeled lookup comes up empty: `COMPANY_SENTENCE_RE`
  (`keycard for <company>.`, anchored to end-of-line so it isn't fooled by
  the mid-sentence "LLC." abbreviation) and `NAME_KEYCARD_LINE_RE` (a bare
  `First Last -1234567890` line).
- Confirms location/serves/access/email/phone should all genuinely come back
  `null` here rather than the parser guessing from the sender's own
  signature block (address, phone, email) - matches example 1's concern
  about not conflating sender info with tenant info.
- Known gap, not yet fixed: this email's signature has no `___`/`--` divider
  line at all, so `stripSignatureBlock()` doesn't cut anything here - the two
  new fallback regexes above just happen not to false-match anywhere in this
  particular signature/NOTICE block. A future example whose divider-less
  signature contains something shaped like a name+number line or a "keycard
  for ..." sentence would need a better signature-boundary heuristic (e.g.
  cutting at a "Name | Title" line, or at the first "NOTICE:"/legal
  boilerplate marker) — hold off generalizing that from a single example.

## Example 3 — Keith Arline III, 218 Machlin Ct, Walnut (9 cardholders in one request)

Raw body (from the dashboard's "View Original Request" modal, truncated - same
sender/format as example 1, but asking for 9 keycards in one email instead of
one):

```
Can I please have 9 key cards activated at 218 Machlin Ct- Walnut

Company Name: Pride Industries (unit A-02)
Access: Warehouse
Facility: 218 Machlin Ct.-Walnut

Cardholder Name: Jason Heard
Keycard Number: 2103651002

Cardholder Name: Ignacio Fonseca
Keycard Number: 2104353626

Cardholder name: Michael Jimenez
Keycard Number: 1838901402

Cardholder Name: Nicole Garcia
...(continues for 9 total pairs)
```

Expected extraction: one "Company - Name - Keycard" line per cardholder, all
sharing the single Company Name/Access/Facility line at the top - not just
the first entry.

Notes for the parser (implemented):
- Added `extractCardholderEntries()` / `CARDHOLDER_KEYCARD_BLOCK_RE`, which
  `matchAll`s every repeated `Cardholder Name: X` immediately followed by
  `Keycard Number: Y` pair (case-insensitive, so "Cardholder name" -
  lowercase n, as actually used for Michael Jimenez here - still matches),
  returned as `cardholderEntries: [{name, keycard}, ...]` alongside the
  existing single-value `cardholderName` (kept as `cardholderEntries[0].name`
  for the existing tag/search code, which predates multi-entry requests).
- The dashboard's "View Original Request" parsed-summary box
  (`buildOriginalRequestSummary` in `app.js`) now repeats the
  "Company - Name - Keycard" line once per entry instead of only ever
  showing the first, falling back to the single `cardholderName`/
  `keycardNumbers[0]` pairing when `cardholderEntries` is empty (e.g.
  example 2's label-less single-entry case, which isn't collected as a
  repeatable block).
- `keycardNumbers` (the flat, order-preserving de-duped list used elsewhere)
  already picked up all 9 numbers before this change, since it's a global
  regex over the whole text - the gap was specifically that nothing paired
  each number back up with *which* cardholder it belonged to.
- Also implemented: the parsed-summary box now flags any keycard number
  shared by more than one entry (`buildOriginalRequestSummary` in `app.js`)
  by rendering it in red with " (duplicate)" appended, since a real example
  turned up two different cardholders (Michael Jimenez / Nicole Garcia)
  pasted in with the exact same keycard number - almost certainly a
  copy/paste mistake in the original email, which is exactly the kind of
  thing this best-effort parsing is meant to surface for a human to catch
  rather than silently paper over.

Known limitation, not yet fixed - per-entry email/phone: `email` and `phone`
are currently single ticket-level fields (one shared value for the whole
message, same as `company`/`access`/`serves`), but in reality each cardholder
entry can have their own email and phone number, same as `name` and
`keycard` already do on `cardholderEntries`. None of the examples so far have
actually included a per-cardholder email/phone in the free-form body (so
there's nothing real yet to pattern-match against), but once one shows up,
`cardholderEntries` should grow `email`/`phone` fields per entry (mirroring
`{ name, keycard }`) instead of relying on the single ticket-level `email`/
`phone` fields, and the dashboard's parsed-summary box
(`buildOriginalRequestSummary` in `app.js`) should show each entry's own
Email/Phone lines rather than one shared Email/Phone line for the whole
ticket. Hold off implementing the actual extraction regex until a real
example shows what labeling/format a per-entry email/phone actually looks
like in these emails.

## Example 4 — Ramon Ontiveros, Mitra EV Inc, 1630 Terminal Street (West Sacramento)

Raw body (from the dashboard's "View Original Request" modal):

```
Please activate Access Key Cards for Mitra EV Inc. for the 1630 Terminal Street location in West Sacramento.


4061247958- Nina Dacanay

4063515910- John Vucasovich
```

Expected extraction:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Tenant Company Name | Mitra EV Inc |
| Cubework Location | 1630 Terminal Street, West Sacramento |
| Entry 1 | Nina Dacanay - 4061247958 |
| Entry 2 | John Vucasovich - 4063515910 |
| Serves / Access / Email / Phone | missing - not stated anywhere |

Notes for the parser (implemented):
- One sentence names BOTH the company and the location together, and uses
  "Key Cards" (two words) rather than "keycard" - neither
  `COMPANY_SENTENCE_RE` nor `extractLocation()`'s labels/`ZIP_ADDRESS_RE`
  (no zip present) could have handled this alone; the plain
  `COMPANY_SENTENCE_RE` in particular would have swallowed the location text
  too, since it just reads to end-of-line. Added a dedicated
  `COMPANY_LOCATION_SENTENCE_RE` (`key card(s) for <company>. for the
  <street> location in <city>.`) tried first, whose street+city groups feed
  `extractLocation()` as a sentence fallback (after labels and
  `ZIP_ADDRESS_RE`, same priority order as every other location source).
- The two entries are bare `1234567890- Name` lines - keycard number
  *first*, reversed from example 2's `Name -1234567890`. Added
  `extractKeycardNameEntries()` / `KEYCARD_NAME_LINE_RE` as a third
  cardholder-entry pattern, tried only when neither the labeled block
  (example 3) nor the name-first bare line (example 2) matched anything.
  Also made the example-2 pattern itself (`NAME_KEYCARD_LINE_RE`) collect
  every match via `matchAll` instead of just the first, so a label-less
  request naming several people that way (not yet seen, but plausible) would
  also come back as multiple `cardholderEntries` rather than just one.
- Confirms serves/access/email/phone should all genuinely come back `null`
  here - nothing in the body states any of them.

## Example 5 — Alen Besic, Restoration Lincoln Park LLC / Paul Glendenning (Cards 19-23)

Subject: `Restoration Lincoln Park LLC - Paul Glendenning Key Card Activation & Charge (Cards 19-23)`

Raw body (from the dashboard's "View Original Request" modal):

```
Hi Huy/Jose,

Please activate the additional key cards for Restoration Lincoln Park at Lincolnwood, same access as their other cards in thread below. Based on my count, this is card numbers 19-23.

3233638676
3169973252
1579618580
4251949326
4252231758

@ar@Novie Boston please charge licensee $150 for 5 additional cards.

Thanks,
Alen Besic | Facilities Manager
```

Expected extraction: a follow-up request for more keycards on an
already-established tenant - same company, same cardholder, 5 more cards
numbered 19 through 23 in order:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Entry 1 | Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #19 - Paul Glendenning - 3233638676 |
| Entry 2 | Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #20 - Paul Glendenning - 3169973252 |
| Entry 3 | Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #21 - Paul Glendenning - 1579618580 |
| Entry 4 | Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #22 - Paul Glendenning - 4251949326 |
| Entry 5 | Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #23 - Paul Glendenning - 4252231758 |

Notes for the parser (implemented):
- Neither the company nor the cardholder name appears anywhere in the body
  at all - "same access as their other cards in thread below" means the
  sender is relying on context from an earlier request instead of restating
  it. The only place both appear is the SUBJECT line itself
  ("`<Company> - <Person> Key Card Activation...`"). Added
  `SUBJECT_COMPANY_NAME_RE`, tried as a fallback source for both `company`
  and `cardholderName` - deliberately narrow (requires "Key Card(s)"
  immediately after the person's name) so it doesn't misfire on examples
  1-4's subjects (verified none of them match).
- This also exposed a real bug in the existing `COMPANY_SENTENCE_RE`
  fallback (example 2's "keycard for X." pattern): the body's sentence here
  is a long run-on ("...key cards for Restoration Lincoln Park at
  Lincolnwood, same access as their other cards in thread below. Based on my
  count, this is card numbers 19-23.") with no clean end-of-line right after
  the company name, so that fallback was grabbing the entire rest of the
  sentence as the "company." Fixed by trying the subject-line match *before*
  that plain sentence fallback, so a reliable subject match wins before the
  risky whole-line grab ever runs.
- The 5 keycard numbers are bare, with no name/label on any of their lines
  at all (unlike every other example) - what makes them attributable to
  Paul Glendenning individually is the body's own "card numbers 19-23"
  range statement, paired positionally with the 5 numbers found. Added
  `CARD_RANGE_RE` + `extractCountedBareKeycardEntries()`, tried only when
  none of the three other entry patterns matched anything, and only when
  the count of bare keycard numbers found exactly equals the range's size
  (19 to 23 inclusive = 5) - a mismatch bails out to `[]` rather than
  guessing, since mislabeling real card numbers would be worse than leaving
  them for a human to sort out.
- Each entry from this pattern carries its own per-entry `company` (e.g.
  `"Restoration Lincoln Park LLC #19"`), the first time an entry differs
  from the shared ticket-level `company`. `buildOriginalRequestSummary` in
  `app.js` now prefers `entry.company` over the shared field when an entry
  sets one.
